Concurrent canvas painter

Northeastern University, 2026

I explored CPU and memory bandwidth limits by adapting a multi-threaded random-painting assignment into a concurrent pipeline that streams canvas snapshots into video.

Painters fill most of the canvas quickly, then spend the rest of the run blocking and backtracking over the last white pixels. A linear frame rate makes that early concurrent progress flash by, but demonstrates the bottleneck of a single thread filling the remaining pixels. a non-linear render rate based on the percent painted creates a satisying visual effect of consistent progress.

In prototyping frames were writen to disk, but I extended the project to stream frames straight into NVIDIA’s H.264 encoder through FFmpeg.

  • One painter thread per logical core, each running a randomized depth-first walk with a backtracking stack.
  • A grid of striped locks with trylock, so painters skip contested pixels instead of waiting on them.
  • A bounded frame queue drained by a dedicated writer thread.

Senior design

Payload protection device

CU Boulder senior design for Sandia National Laboratories, 2020–2021

As lead CAD engineer, I led design and sensor integration for a device that rides in a model rocket’s nose cone, records its flight dynamics, and protects the data through extreme cold and hard landings.

  • Stacked TinyDuino sensor boards inside molded polyurethane impact foam and melamine thermal insulation.
  • Characterized the foam on a universal testing machine and a drop tower, then used the stress-strain data for non-linear simulation in SolidWorks.
  • Verified pressure, temperature, acceleration, and shock ranges in pressure chambers, a furnace, dry ice, and on a vibration table.
  • Won Best Experimental Design and Validation from Sandia among six universities.
